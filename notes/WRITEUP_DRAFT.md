## Design rationale

**The question drives the layout.** We wanted to answer two things at once: how much a given city warms, and
how much of that warming depends on which emissions pathway the world follows. Every view is driven by the same
two controls, pathway and decade, so changing one updates the map, the ranking, the headline sentence and the
time series together.

**Map.** We used an Equal Earth projection because it preserves area. Mercator would make the Arctic, where
warming is largest, look far bigger than it is. Temperature change is shaded with a single-family sequential
scale (light to dark red), because warming is a magnitude, not a positive/negative split. We fixed the scale at
0–9 °C for every pathway and decade. We considered rescaling per view, but that would make SSP1-2.6 look as dramatic as
SSP5-8.5. With a fixed scale, the low pathway looks pale because it really is mild. City dots use the same scale
so they read as samples of the field around them.

**Ranking.** The map is good for "where" but bad for comparing exact amounts, because colour is a weak channel
for magnitude. So the ranking puts all 68 cities on one horizontal axis (position, the strongest channel) with
one dot per pathway. The grey bar between a city's lowest and highest dot is the "how much is still a choice"
part of our question. We considered a grouped bar chart, but four bars × 68 cities was too dense. Dots on a
shared line compare more easily.

**Pathway colours.** The four pathways are ordered from low to high emissions, so we used an ordered palette:
blue, gold, orange, dark red. We checked it with a colour-blindness simulator and adjusted it until adjacent
pathways stayed distinguishable. Gold has weak contrast on a white background, so the pathways are also always
named in text: legend, tooltips and line end labels.

**Time series.** For the comparison we used small multiples, one panel per city with a shared y-axis, instead of
overlaying cities. Four cities × four pathways would have been 16 lines on one chart. Inside a panel, colour
always means pathway. We show the model range only for the selected pathway, since four overlapping bands were
unreadable. A dashed global-mean line in each panel answers "is this city warming faster than the planet?"

**Interaction.**
- *Pathway toggle and decade slider (with play)*: the core dynamic query. Playing through the decades shows the
  dots in the ranking spreading apart as the pathways diverge, which a static chart can't show well.
- *Region filter*: narrows the ranking, dims the other cities on the map, and recomputes the headline.
- *Sort by warming / pathway gap / latitude*: each sort reveals a different pattern. Latitude shows Arctic
  amplification, and gap shows where the pathway choice matters most.
- *Zoom and pan*: European and East Asian cities overlap at full-world scale.
- *Details on demand*: hovering anywhere on the map shows that grid cell's value, and hovering a city shows all
  four pathways with the model range and the absolute temperature change.
- *Click to compare* (up to 4 cities, like NameGrapher's selection).
- *Guided stories*: four buttons set the controls to show a specific finding with a one-line explanation, so a
  first-time reader has a starting point before exploring.

median and range across 9 models → decade means for the map and ranking, 9-year running mean for the lines.
